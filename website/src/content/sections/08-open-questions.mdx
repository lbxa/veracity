The paper closes by treating the framework as a research agenda rather than a finished protocol. Its open questions are not footnotes to the model; they are the places where an implementation would have to make hard choices.

The framework also needs policies for claims that remain unchallenged. Absence of a dispute may mean the claim is correct, but it may also mean no qualified challenger saw it, the claim was too trivial to contest, or the audience lacked the resources to evaluate it. Future implementations should make that uncertainty visible.

The paper&apos;s most promising contribution is the shift in framing: trust is not only a label applied after publication. It can be designed as an incentive system in which creators, challengers, jurors, viewers, identity infrastructure, and provenance standards all participate.
